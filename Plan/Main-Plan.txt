Yes — reviewing the research, I would **change the app architecture so SIP/VoIP is the primary calling method**, not an optional add-on. That gives the AI direct access to the incoming and outgoing audio streams and avoids Android’s hard restriction on capturing/injecting normal cellular-call audio. The research supports that distinction clearly: normal Android apps can control cellular calls, but autonomous bidirectional audio is practical when the app owns the SIP/VoIP media path. 

## Recommended architecture

The app should be a completely self-contained Android AI calling client:

```text
Android App
│
├── Onboarding / Device Qualification
│   ├── Hardware check
│   ├── Storage check
│   ├── RAM check
│   ├── Android/API check
│   ├── CPU/GPU capability test
│   ├── Model benchmark
│   └── Download required AI models
│
├── User-configured VoIP client
│   ├── SIP account
│   ├── SIP username
│   ├── SIP password
│   ├── SIP domain / registrar
│   ├── outbound proxy
│   ├── transport: UDP/TCP/TLS
│   ├── STUN/TURN
│   ├── codecs
│   └── connection test
│
├── SIP/RTP Calling Engine
│   ├── SIP signaling
│   ├── RTP/SRTP audio
│   ├── jitter buffer
│   ├── codec decode
│   ├── codec encode
│   ├── DTMF
│   └── Android ConnectionService
│
├── Local AI Pipeline
│   ├── VAD
│   ├── ASR / speech-to-text
│   ├── Gemma 4 E2B
│   ├── conversation controller
│   ├── safety/action validator
│   └── local TTS
│
└── UI
    ├── Create call task
    ├── Business/number
    ├── What should AI ask?
    ├── Call screen
    ├── Live transcription
    ├── Take over
    ├── End call
    └── Result summary
```

The important part is that **the user's own VoIP provider handles the telephone connectivity**. Your servers do not need to operate a telephone network, buy numbers, process the conversation, or run an AI service.

The app would essentially behave like a normal SIP softphone with an AI sitting directly inside its local audio pipeline.

| Free storage          | **At least 6 GB**                  |

These are engineering minimums rather than Google's official requirements; the research recommends roughly 8 GB RAM as the practical floor for Gemma 4 E2B and 12 GB as the preferred level.

The app should benchmark the actual phone.

# 9. SIP library

Do not write a SIP stack yourself.

Use a mature SIP/VoIP implementation.

The architecture should hide the implementation behind:

```kotlin
interface VoipClient {

    val registrationState:
        StateFlow<RegistrationState>

    val callState:
        StateFlow<CallState>

    suspend fun register(
        config: SipAccount
    )

    suspend fun call(
        destination: String
    )

    suspend fun hangup()

    suspend fun sendDtmf(
        digit: Char
    )

    val incomingAudio:
        Flow<PcmFrame>

    suspend fun sendAudio(
        frame: PcmFrame
    )
}
```

The AI code should **never know anything about SIP**.

It sees PCM audio.

# 12. The conversation controller

I would not allow Gemma to independently control everything.

Have a deterministic state machine:

```text
IDLE
↓
PREPARING
↓
DIALING
↓
RINGING
↓
ANSWERED
↓
DISCLOSURE
↓
ASKING
↓
LISTENING
↓
THINKING
↓
RESPONDING
↓
LISTENING
     ↓
ANSWER_RECEIVED
↓
CONFIRMING
↓
GOODBYE
↓
COMPLETE
```

Gemma supplies **decisions**, not unrestricted commands.

Example:

```json
{
    "action": "SPEAK",
    "speech": "Does that price include balancing?",
    "confidence": 0.94,
    "reasonCode": "PRICE_CLARIFICATION"
}
```

Allowed actions:

```text
SPEAK
LISTEN
CLARIFY
SEND_DTMF
FINISH
HANDOFF
ABORT
```

# 15. Barge-in support

If the business starts talking while the AI is talking:

```text
remote speech detected
↓
stop TTS immediately
↓
clear unsent TTS audio
↓
listen
```

Target interruption time:

**under ~250 ms**.

The research recommends beginning with controlled half-duplex plus interruption rather than trying to make both sides speak freely simultaneously.

# 27. One architectural decision I would change from the original research

The research presents both:

```text
PSTN cellular control
and
SIP autonomous calling
```

as major portions of the application. 

For **this app**, I wouldn't spend much development time on becoming the phone's default cellular dialer initially.

Your core use case is:

> **AI calls a business and independently talks to them.**

Cellular doesn't give us the media streams needed to accomplish that.

Therefore make:

### Primary product

**User-supplied SIP/VoIP account → AI autonomous calling**

And potentially later add:

### Secondary feature

**SIM call → human makes call with AI assistance**

That keeps development focused on the feature you actually care about.

## Development order

The most important thing is **not to start by building the pretty UI or even Gemma integration**.

Build it in this order:

1. **SIP client that can successfully call a real telephone number.**
2. **Get decoded incoming PCM and inject generated PCM into the call.**
3. **Local TTS → SIP** and verify the person can hear it clearly.
4. **SIP → local ASR** and verify business speech is transcribed.
5. **Add Gemma 4 E2B.**
6. **Add the deterministic agent state machine.**
7. **Add DTMF/IVR handling, barge-in and user takeover.**
8. **Build model download/verification management.**
9. **Build the hardware qualification benchmark.**
10. **Wrap everything in the polished onboarding and call UI.**

If **#2 works reliably**, the hardest architectural problem has effectively been solved. Everything after that becomes normal Android/audio/ML engineering rather than fighting Android's cellular telephony restrictions.
